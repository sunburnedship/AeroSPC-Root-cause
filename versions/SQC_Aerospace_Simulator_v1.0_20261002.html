Mission Briefing — Meridian Aerospace, Plant 7

You are the Quality Engineering lead for the production line that machines and inspects Ni-superalloy turbine blades and assembles avionics flight-control boards for a next-gen regional jet. Every lot you release is flight-critical. Across 6 rounds you will, each time: (A) read the SPC data and decide whether to continue production or stop the line; (B) if you stop, review the station log and pick the assignable cause behind the signal; then (C) watch fresh data to see whether your fix actually cleared the problem. Stopping a stable process wastes uptime; running through a real signal ships defective hardware — both show up in your quality-cost tally.

The control charts you will use

X̄ & R charts — variables data
Variables data from machining, pressure, and dimensional checks. X̄ catches shifts in the mean; R catches shifts in variability / spread. Limits use the standard A₂, D₃, D₄ subgroup factors.
p-chart — fraction defective
Solder-joint pass/fail on avionics boards. Used when you can count defectives and the sample size, so a proportion p̂ can be computed.
c-chart — count of defects
Surface flaws per coated blade. Used when you can count defects but not a meaningful proportion. UCL/LCL = c̄ ± 3√c̄.
Reading the signal
A point past 3σ, a run of 8+ on one side, a 6+ point trend, or 2-of-3 past 2σ all flag a special cause. No signal → continue. Real signal → stop, diagnose, fix, and re-verify.

The equations (from your handout)

X̄: CL = x̿
UCL/LCL = x̿ ± A₂·R̄
R: CL = R̄
UCL_R = D₄·R̄
LCL_R = D₃·R̄
p: CL = p̄
σ_p = √(p̄(1−p̄)/n)
UCL/LCL = p̄ ± 3σ_p (LCL≥0)
c: CL = c̄
UCL/LCL = c̄ ± 3√c̄ (LCL≥0)

Western Electric run rules you'll apply

Beyond a single point past 3σ, watch for: 8 consecutive points on one side of the centerline (mean shift), a steady trend of 6+ rising/falling points (tool wear / drift), and 2-of-3 points beyond 2σ (emerging instability). These distinguish real special-cause signals from random common-cause noise.

You'll run 10 rounds, each drawn at random from a large bank of aerospace production scenarios. The mix of X̄-R, p-, and c-charts — and which are stable vs. faulty — changes every shift, so no two runs are alike.
